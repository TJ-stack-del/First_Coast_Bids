## Product Overview
**One-liner:** Done-for-you compliance paperwork for small trade contractors bidding on local/state government contracts.
**What it does:** A client uploads an RFP/solicitation; a real person on First Coast Bids' team turns it into a ready-to-submit capability statement, compliance matrix, and technical narrative, tailored to the client's trade and the agency's actual requirements. The client reviews, signs, and submits it themselves through their own vendor portal — First Coast Bids never touches the agency relationship or the client's credentials.
**Product category:** Done-for-you government-bid preparation service for small businesses (not a bid-writing SaaS tool, not a lead-gen/opportunity-matching platform, though it does light opportunity matching as a feature).
**Product type:** Service business with software tooling behind it (client portal + admin ops platform), not self-serve SaaS.
**Business model:** Manually confirmed, invoiced pricing (no self-serve checkout). Three packages: **Pilot** (a low-commitment first bid, on us, to prove the process — no commitment after), **One-off** (a single bid, fully prepared), **Retainer** (ongoing coverage, up to 2 full bids/month). Below a configurable dollar threshold (org-set, default informed by FL Statute 287.017 Category Two), a "lean package" (Rate Sheet + Executive Cover + Certificate of Insurance) is offered instead of the full 3-deliverable set.

## Target Audience
**Target companies:** Small trade contractors and service businesses — sole proprietors up through small crews — who occasionally or regularly bid on local/state government contracts.
**Decision-makers:** The owner/operator themselves in almost all cases (not a procurement department — these are small businesses where the owner does the bidding, the labor, and the paperwork).
**Primary use case:** "I found a government bid I could actually do the work for, but I don't have the time or expertise to produce a compliant submission package before the deadline."
**Jobs to be done:**
- Turn a dense, jargon-heavy RFP into a complete, compliant bid package without hiring bid-writing staff
- Know whether a given bid is actually a good fit for the business before investing time in it
- Avoid getting disqualified on a technicality (missing form, wrong certification claim, missed mandatory site visit)
**Use cases:**
- A janitorial contractor bidding on a city facilities-maintenance contract
- An HVAC contractor responding to a county RFP with a tight page limit and specific bonding requirements
- A landscaping or electrical contractor who wants to start bidding on government work but has never done it before and doesn't know where to start

## Personas
Single-persona business (see Target Audience/Decision-makers) — the owner is user, champion, decision maker, and financial buyer in nearly every case. No multi-stakeholder B2B buying committee to model.

## Problems & Pain Points
**Core problem:** Government RFPs bury a straightforward trade job under compliance matrices, capability statement requirements, insurance/bonding minimums, prevailing-wage language, and agency-specific rules (SIDA badging for airports, background checks for schools, DBE participation for transit, etc.) — none of which a trade contractor was ever trained on.
**Why alternatives fall short:**
- Doing it themselves costs a full busy season's worth of attention they don't have, and a single missed technical requirement can get a bid rejected as non-responsive before it's even evaluated on merit
- Generic AI writing tools will confidently fabricate a certification number, a past-project detail, or a compliance claim that isn't true — dangerous to submit to a government agency
- Hiring a full-time proposal writer or bid consultant is not viable at this company size/deal size
**What it costs them:** A real contract they were qualified to win, lost not on price or capability but on paperwork; or the owner's own billable time diverted from running jobs during their busiest season.
**Emotional tension:** Feeling like the paperwork itself — not the work — is the real barrier to growing into government contracting; distrust of "AI-written" documents that might contain confident-sounding fabrications they'd be liable for submitting.

## Differentiation
**Key differentiators:**
- A real person prepares and reviews every deliverable — not a fully automated pipeline
- Never fabricates a fact: any detail not explicitly provided by the client renders as a visible bracketed placeholder rather than an invented value, and a client can't download a packet with unresolved placeholders in it
- Real, agency-specific compliance detection (school → background checks, airport → SIDA badging, transit → DBE goals, VA → CUI/508 requirements, detention → PREA/bloodborne training) derived from the agency name and the RFP's own text — not generic boilerplate
- Compliance-matrix rows extracted directly from the uploaded RFP now carry the exact page number and verbatim quoted source sentence, so a human reviewer verifies against the real document without re-reading all of it
- A "fit check" readiness signal (strong/moderate/weak) scoped honestly to "is this submission well-formed," never framed as a win-probability
- 48-hour typical turnaround; no card required to start; the client previews a real excerpt of every deliverable for free before deciding to move forward, with the complete package unlocking once payment is confirmed
**How we do it differently:** Combines real human bid-prep expertise with narrow, deterministic tooling (document extraction, compliance-rule detection, placeholder-enforcement) instead of either fully manual work or an unsupervised AI writing the whole thing.
**Why that's better:** The client gets speed and affordability closer to an AI tool, with the trustworthiness of a human-reviewed, fact-checked submission.
**Why customers choose us:** They're bidding on public money with their name and credentials on the line — accuracy and compliance risk outweigh saving a few more dollars on a purely automated option.

## Objections
| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "Can you guarantee I'll win?" | "No one can guarantee an award. What we guarantee is a complete, compliant submission prepared by people who've done this before." |
| "Is my data secure?" | "Your submission and files are tied to your account only, and every status change on your bid is recorded in an audit trail." |
| "How much does this cost?" | "One-off starts at $399, Retainer starts at $649/mo, and Pilot is free for our first 10 clients. We confirm the exact number with you directly before any work starts — no card required to get started, and every deliverable is free to preview before anything's due." |
| "What do I need to get started?" | "Just the RFP itself (or a link to it) and basic company info — NAICS codes, small-business status, and set-asides if you have them; the rest can be filled in later." |

**Anti-persona:** Large contractors with an in-house proposal/compliance team; businesses looking for opportunity-sourcing/lead-gen as the primary need rather than bid preparation; anyone expecting a fully-automated, no-human-review AI output.

## Switching Dynamics
**Push:** A missed or nearly-missed deadline; a bid rejected as non-responsive over a technicality; the sheer dread of another RFP landing during a busy season.
**Pull:** No card required to start, a real person handling the actual paperwork, and previewing real excerpts of the actual work before committing to anything.
**Habit:** Either not bidding on government work at all, or muddling through it themselves with whatever time is left after real jobs.
**Anxiety:** Trusting an unfamiliar company with a real, must-submit compliance document; worry that an AI-assisted product will quietly invent a fact that gets them in legal trouble with an agency.

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|

| Compliance matrix | Row-by-row mapping of RFP requirements to how the bid meets them, with verification status |
| Technical narrative | The operational-approach deliverable describing how the work will actually be performed |
| Fit check | Automated strong/moderate/weak readiness signal shown after a bid is submitted |
| Lean package | The reduced 3-document set (Rate Sheet, Executive Cover, Certificate of Insurance) offered for smaller-value bids |
